## 🧭 Educational Philosophy: "Curiosity Over Fear"

Instead of making children afraid of the outdoors, this curriculum empowers students with **scientific literacy, observational skills, and civic agency**. Ticks are treated not as monsters, but as fascinating biological puzzles with predictable physics, chemistry, and ecology that students can understand, measure, and manage.

# 📚 Part 1: 5 Interactive Classroom Lesson Plans

---

### 🔬 Lesson 1: "The Microscopic Hitchhiker" (Arachnid Biology & Anatomy)

* **Big Question**: Why is a tick not an insect, and how does it hold on so tight?
* **Core Concepts**:
  * **Anatomy**: 8 legs (arachnid, cousin of spiders), zero antennae, two body regions (capitulum and idiosoma).
  * **The Hypostome**: The tick mouthpart is covered in backward-facing microscopic barbs (like a harpoon or shark tooth) and glues itself in place with a biological cement protein!
  * **Haller's Organ**: A tiny chemical sensory pit on the front feet that smells carbon dioxide ($CO_2$) and body warmth from 10 feet away.
* **Hands-On Classroom Activity**:
  * **"The Velcro & Harpoon Challenge"**: Students examine high-res macro photos of the barbed hypostome under classroom stereoscopes or digital loupes. Students use backward-facing Velcro strips against foam to test why pulling *straight up with fine tweezers* works, while twisting or squishing breaks the mouthparts.
* **Exit Ticket Question**: *"Why does a 6-legged baby tick (larva) not transmit Lyme disease?"*  
  *(Answer: Lyme bacteria are not passed through tick eggs; larvae only pick up bacteria after their first meal on an infected mouse).*

---

### ⏱️ Lesson 2: "The 36-Hour Clock: Bacteria on the Move" (Microbiology)

* **Big Question**: Why do doctors have a "72-hour safety window" to prevent Lyme disease?
* **Core Concepts**:
  * **The Spirochete Shape**: *Borrelia burgdorferi* is shaped like a tiny corkscrew that wiggles through tissues.
  * **The OspA to OspC Molecular Switch**:
    * When cold in the grass ($15^\circ\text{C}$), the bacteria sleep inside the tick’s stomach anchored by a protein glue called **OspA**.
    * When warm human blood ($37^\circ\text{C}$) enters, the bacteria wake up, detach from OspA, turn on **OspC**, and crawl up to the salivary glands.
    * **This journey takes 36 to 48 hours!**
* **Hands-On Classroom Activity**:
  * **"The Poppy Seed to Raisin Scale"**: Students hold real physical scale models:
    * Flat Poppy Seed ($1.0\text{ mm}$, $<24\text{h}$ attached $\to$ Safe!)
    * Sesame Seed ($1.8\text{ mm}$, $24\text{–}36\text{h}$)
    * Small Lentil ($2.5\text{ mm}$, $36\text{–}48\text{h} \to$ Doctor threshold)
    * Plump Raisin ($5.0\text{ mm}$, $>48\text{h}$)
* **Exit Ticket Question**: *"If you find a flat poppy-seed tick on your sock after a hike, why are you safe from Lyme?"*

---

### 🌿 Lesson 3: "The Island Ecosystem Web: Mice, Deer & Barberry" (Ecology)

* **Big Question**: Why are there 12 times more ticks inside a prickly Japanese Barberry bush than in native woods?
* **Core Concepts**:
  * **The Trophic Web**:
    * **White-Footed Mice**: The main reservoir where young ticks catch Lyme.
    * **White-Tailed Deer**: The meeting ground where adult ticks mate.
    * **Turkeys & Towhees**: Helpful ground predators that eat ticks!
  * **The Barberry Microclimate Umbrella**:
    * Japanese Barberry (*Berberis thunbergii*) branches form dense, humid tents that trap 80–90% humidity at ground level.
    * Deer won’t eat spiny barberry, so it crowds out native blueberries and ferns.
    * Mice build safe nests under barberry thorns where owls and hawks can't reach them!
* **Hands-On Classroom Activity**:
  * **"Microclimate Chamber Lab"**: Students compare two terrariums with digital hygrometers—one with open sandy soil and one with dense mulch and umbrella cover—observing how quickly relative humidity drops when wind is blown over open trails.

---

### 💻 Lesson 4: "Citizen Science in Action: Zooniverse Lab" (Data Science)

* **Big Question**: How can 6th graders help MIT and Nantucket park rangers map island biodiversity?
* **Classroom Workflow**:
  * Students log into the **Island Tick Detectives** project on Zooniverse.org.
  * In teams of two, students classify 10 mystery macro specimens:
    1. Count legs (6 vs. 8).
    2. Check the shield (Dark = Deer tick; Racing stripes = Dog tick; White dot = Lone star).
    3. Measure the Scutal Index ($SI = \frac{\text{Body Length}}{\text{Shield Width}}$) using the digital caliper tool.
  * Students log results onto a shared classroom GIS map of Nantucket trails.

---

### 🌟 Lesson 5: "Seven Generations Island Stewardship" (Civics & Solutions)

* **Big Question**: What decisions can we make today so Nantucket trails remain healthy 150 years into the future?
* **Core Concepts**:
  * **The Seven Generations Principle**: Rooted in indigenous Wampanoag and Haudenosaunee philosophy—every action taken today must consider its impact on grandchildren seven generations ahead.
  * **Integrated Pest Management (IPM)**:
    * Trail widening & mowing (6ft buffers so clothing never brushes tall grass).
    * Planting native Northern Bayberry & Sweet Fern.
    * Supporting ethical research like MIT’s "Mice Against Ticks" engineered immunity.

# ✍️ Part 2: 4 Student Civic Action Letter Templates

These letter prompts guide students to practice persuasive writing, citing empirical evidence from their science lessons.

### 🏛️ Letter 1: To the Nantucket Town Select Board & Board of Health
**Purpose**: Advocating for wider mowed trail buffers and free tick tweezers at school bus stops.

```
[Date]

To the Nantucket Town Select Board & Board of Health
Town and County of Nantucket
16 Broad Street, Nantucket, MA 02554

Dear Members of the Select Board and Health Commissioners,

My name is [Student Name], and I am a [6th/7th/8th] grade student at [Nantucket Middle School / School Name]. In our science class, we have been studying the biology and ecology of ticks on our island as part of the Island Tick Detectives project.

We learned that blacklegged ticks (Ixodes scapularis) quest for hosts by perching on tall grasses right next to walking paths. Because ticks dry out quickly when exposed to sunlight and ocean breezes (Vapor Pressure Deficit > 1.0 kPa), they cannot cross wide, sunny dirt shoulders.

I am writing to respectfully ask the Town to support two simple safety steps:
1. Maintain a 6-foot wide mowed buffer zone along all municipal walking paths, bike paths, and school bus stops.
2. Install weatherproof tick-check signs with fine-tipped tweezers and magnifying cards at major public trailheads like Sanford Farm and Tom Nevers.

Our science research shows that removing a tick within 36 hours prevents Lyme disease. By keeping our trail edges trimmed and giving hikers tools to inspect themselves immediately, we can protect island families while keeping our beautiful moors open for everyone.

Thank you for your leadership and for protecting our island for the next seven generations.

Sincerely,

[Student Signature]
[Student Printed Name]
Grade [6], [Class / Teacher Name]
```

### 🌿 Letter 2: To Island Landscapers & Garden Centers
**Purpose**: Asking nurseries to stop planting invasive Japanese Barberry and promote native Bayberry.

```
[Date]

To Nantucket Landscape Professionals and Garden Nurseries,

Dear Island Gardeners and Landscape Stewards,

My name is [Student Name], and I am an Island Tick Detective from [School Name]. Our science class recently completed a study on how non-native plants affect tick populations on Nantucket.

We studied research from the Connecticut Agricultural Experiment Station (Williams & Ward, 2010), which discovered that invasive Japanese Barberry (Berberis thunbergii) creates a dense humidity dome (80–90% RH) that acts like a "tick hotel." Forest areas with barberry have over 120 Lyme-infected ticks per acre, compared to only 10 per acre in native woods.

As young island stewards, we kindly ask you to join our Seven Generations Island Pledge:
• Please stop selling or planting invasive Japanese Barberry and Asian Bittersweet in island yards.
• Recommend native, tick-safe plants to your clients, such as Northern Bayberry (Morella pensylvanica), Sweet Fern (Comptonia peregrina), and Highbush Blueberry.

Native plants protect our sandplain grasslands, support native songbirds, and keep our backyards safer for kids and pets. Thank you for helping us keep Nantucket beautiful and healthy!

Warm regards,

[Student Signature]
[Student Name]
[School / Youth Organization]
```

### 🚢 Letter 3: Welcome Letter to Summer Visitors & Ferry Passengers
**Purpose**: A student-written friendly guide handed out on Steamship Authority & Hy-Line ferries.

```
Welcome to Nantucket Island! 🌊
A Friendly Trail Guide from the Island Tick Detectives

Dear Island Visitor,

Welcome to our home! Nantucket has some of the most stunning beaches, bike trails, and open moors in the world. As local students, we want to make sure your vacation is full of fun, wonder, and zero tick bites!

Here are our top 4 "Detective Tips" for your island adventures:

1. 🧦 The Sock Trick: Tuck your pants into light-colored socks when walking on dirt trails. Ticks start at your shoes and crawl upward—tucking your pants keeps them on the outside where you can spot them instantly!
2. 🚶 Stay in the Center: Walk in the middle of paths and avoid brushing against tall beach grass or dense shrubs.
3. ⏱️ The 60-Second Shower Check: After every beach or nature walk, do a quick ankle-to-hairline check. Check behind knees, around beltlines, and behind ears.
4. 🔎 Found a Tiny Bug? Don't panic! A flat poppy-seed tick attached for less than 24 hours cannot transmit Lyme disease. Use pointy tweezers to pull straight up, wash with soap, and take a photo for our Zooniverse research lab!

Have an amazing visit, enjoy the moors, and thank you for helping us take care of Nantucket!

From your island friends,
The Students of [School Name] & Nantucket Conservation Foundation
```

## 📋 Classroom Evaluation & Rubric

| Criteria | Developing (1) | Proficient (2) | Exemplary (3) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Scientific Accuracy** | Mentions ticks without distinguishing species or life stages. | Correctly identifies deer ticks, 8 legs, and the 36-hour attachment window. | Accurately explains OspA/OspC molecular switch, Scutal Index, and microclimate desiccation. |
| **Data & Evidence Use** | States opinions without citing facts or numbers. | Cites basic stats (e.g., 50% infection rate, 36h rule). | Integrates peer-reviewed data (Williams 2010 barberry, Nadelman 2001 prophylaxis) into letters. |
| **Civic Voice & Respect** | Demanding or informal tone. | Clear, polite request to town leaders or landscapers. | Inspiring, persuasive, and grounded in the Seven Generations stewardship philosophy. |
